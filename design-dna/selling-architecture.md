# Selling architecture — the conversion codex

Catalogued 2026-07-14 from two live teardowns Jake picked: acquisition.com
(pure selling machinery, visuals irrelevant) and gemini.google's video page
(show-then-tell perfected). Companion to apple-grammar.md (which owns layout
and attention); this file owns PERSUASION ORDER. Both references were read
from their rendered DOM, section by section.

## The two poles, one discipline

- Gemini: show → show → show/tell → show/tell → TELL → FAQ. Quality is
  never argued in text; demos are the rebuttal. One sentence per section.
- Acquisition: ask → qualify → justify → ask, with proof placed AROUND the
  asks. Almost nothing is shown; everything is claimed with dollar-specific
  numbers and then gated.
- Shared spine: ONE repeated CTA string, objections answered exactly where
  they arise, absence as strategy (no brochure nav, no generic contact
  funnel, no urgency theater).

## The laws (with the receipts)

1. **The CTA never changes clothes.** Acquisition uses the exact string
   "I'M READY TO SCALE" at every ask — even immediately after the $5k price
   reveal. Gemini repeats "Try Gemini Omni" into one URL. Identity-shaped
   wording ("I'm ready", not "Learn more") collapses the gap between
   interest and commitment. EAS: one first-person phrase, verbatim,
   sitewide; secondary re-asks stay quiet text-chevrons (Apple grammar).

2. **Qualify proudly; rejection is marketing.** "If you are under $250k/yr
   this is not a fit (yet.)" — the filter is founder-voiced, quantified,
   and softened with "yet" so the disqualified stay in orbit. Naming who
   it's NOT for is what makes "for you" believable. High-ticket REQUIRES
   this.

3. **Justify the price before you state it.** Acquisition justifies $5k via
   what their directors earn, then closes with self-disqualification ("If
   you haven't made at least $5k from the free content, don't do it").
   General form: the arithmetic of why-it-costs-that sits ADJACENT to the
   number, never in another section.

4. **Proof placement is inverted from the default.** Authority bio comes
   AFTER the asks (a closer for non-converters, not an opener). Testimonials
   sit immediately AFTER the first CTA — catching the hoverer who hesitated.

5. **The give doubles as the qualifier.** Free courses build trust AND
   become the entry bar ("made $5k from the free stuff"). A free teardown/
   audit does the same for an agency: value forward, and only serious
   owners hand over their ad account.

6. **Show before every tell; one sentence per section.** Gemini: headlines
   ≤6 words, imperative fragments with echo ("Speak it. See it. Share it."),
   zero captions on individual demos, and the single copy-heavy "rational"
   section arrives only AFTER four straight demo sections — justification
   after the emotional sell, never instead of it.

7. **Objections die where they're born.** Paywall disclosed in one line
   directly under the hero CTA; avatar privacy answered inside the avatar
   answer; everything else collapsed in a last-position FAQ. Never a
   generic "trust us" section.

8. **Absence is strategy.** Acquisition: no pricing on the homepage, no
   services menu, no team page, no contact form, no named case studies —
   every path is free-value or qualified application. Gemini: no
   testimonials, no comparison table, no urgency mechanics, no email gate.
   Every element the page DOESN'T have removes a decision.

9. **Numbers, never adjectives.** $250m+, $46.2M exit, 1M copies, $350k/
   $600k/$900k directors, $250k/yr floor. Even the rejection is quantified.
   Vague adjectives are where trust leaks.

## Where EAS deliberately diverges

- **We publish prices; they hide them.** Transparency IS the EAS filter and
  differentiator (the estimator, the sheet). Keep. Law 3 still applies —
  justification adjacent to every number.
- **We keep a contact path** (local trust for Mesa buyers) — but every
  primary ask funnels to the ONE action (the estimate).
- **No urgency theater, ever** (consistent with Apple grammar and the
  anti-slop list).

## EAS applications (ranked 2026-07-14)

1. One CTA string sitewide — "Price my project" (built).
2. "Who we're not for" filter block on /pricing (built).
3. Proof-adjacency audit: a proof beat right AFTER each ask.
4. The qualifying give: the instant site check on the homepage (built —
   automated, truthful findings only; the "human read" escalation link
   is the manual-teardown path and spends Jake's time).
5. Show-then-tell order pass on service pages (demo leads every section;
   rational block last).
